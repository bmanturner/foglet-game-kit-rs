## 2. Back of the box blurb

### Drop Dead Nebula

The relay lights are going out.

You are the captain of a second-hand ship with a cracked transponder, a cargo hold that still smells like reactor coolant, and one clean chance to make your name before the nebula eats it.

Welcome to the Drop Dead Nebula, a collapsing frontier of dead gates, ruined convoys, hungry stations, pirate clans, black-market brokers, machine prophets, and wreck fields that still whisper on emergency bands.

Every call to the BBS gives you a fresh day of turns. Spend them well. Haul Med Gel to a station running on sparks. Smuggle restricted goods through customs. Race another captain to a derelict before its orbit decays. Take a bounty. Plant a mine. Buy low, sell high, lie better, run faster.

And then log off.

When you come back, the nebula has moved without you. Markets shift. NPC captains undercut your route. Factions advance their schemes. Patrols tighten. Pirates leave toll buoys. Someone found a wreck you were saving for tomorrow.

There is no pause button in a dying frontier.

#### Features shouted from the box

- Trade volatile goods across a dangerous directed-sector map.
- Spend limited daily turns in fast 5 to 15 minute BBS sessions.
- Build your reputation as trader, smuggler, salvager, privateer, faction agent, pirate, or disaster magnet.
- Read notices, rumors, route warnings, bounty posts, and station news every time you dock.
- Watch the shared world change through durable events, NPC activity, market drift, faction projects, and world ticks.
- Board derelicts, recover black boxes, and sell the pieces nobody admits they need.
- Choose sides in the struggle for the Ash Mercy Corridor and beyond.
- Leave scars: wrecks, price shocks, grudges, outpost stockpiles, planted traps, rescued stations, and unfinished business.

#### The promise

Drop Dead Nebula is not a space game that waits politely for you.

It is a door that opens into trouble.

## 5. Manual introduction

### Welcome, captain

If this manual came in the box, it would have a crease through the middle and a coffee ring over the ship table.

Good. You are going to need both.

Drop Dead Nebula is a frontier game about bad routes and worse timing. You are not a chosen hero. You are a captain with a ship, a few turns, a local station account, and a name that can still be ruined.

The game is played in short daily sessions. You log in, read the boards, decide what today is for, spend your turns, and leave the universe in a slightly different shape than you found it. Maybe you delivered medicine. Maybe you dumped coolant to crash a market. Maybe you ran from a patrol and left a wreck marker behind you. Maybe you helped repair a relay that your enemies will use tomorrow.

That is the nebula. Nobody gets clean hands. Some people get paid.

## 6. Faction lore from the manual

### 6.1 Mourning Union

#### Manual entry

The Mourning Union began as a dockworkers' burial fund after the first relay collapse. Too many crews died in dark corridors. Too many stations marked missing ships as "delayed" to keep insurance costs down. The Union started counting bodies because nobody else would.

Now they run repair yards, refugee manifests, emergency med bays, labor offices, and quiet memorial walls in ports that officially have no dead.

Union captains haul Med Gel, Reactor Coolant, pressure sealant, food bricks, spare antennas, and coffin tags. They patch stations nobody else can profit from. They keep routes open long enough for civilians to flee. They also break knees when a broker withholds relief cargo.

The Union likes captains who deliver what they promised, rescue crews, repair relays, and keep their guns off refugee traffic. They hate claim-jumpers, pirates, price gougers, and anyone who sells medicine back to a dying station at famine rates.

#### What they want

- Mercy Relay repaired and kept online.
- Refugee corridors marked and defended.
- Relief cargo protected from pirates and speculators.
- Dead crews recorded instead of erased.

#### What they offer

- Repair discounts.
- Relief contracts.
- Safer docking at Union-friendly stations.
- Rescue notices before the public board sees them.
- A reputation that can get you fed when your credits cannot.

#### Union saying

```text
Bring them home breathing. Failing that, bring the nameplate.
```

#### First contact

```text
MOURNING UNION DESK - ASH COIL

We do not care what your ship looks like.
We care whether it arrives.

Mercy Relay needs Med Gel before the next brownout.
Take the run, captain. Then we will talk about better work.
```

### 6.2 Helix Cartel

#### Manual entry

The Helix Cartel calls itself a trade consortium when inspectors are close enough to hear. Everyone else calls it what it is: a braid of brokers, smugglers, debt clerks, bonded warehouses, auction ghosts, and station officials who discovered that scarcity is more profitable than abundance.

The Cartel does not own the Ash Mercy Corridor. It owns favors along it. A loading door left unlocked. A patrol schedule copied twice. A customs seal that passes inspection because the inspector's nephew needs surgery. A rumor placed exactly where a desperate captain will find it.

The Cartel likes captains who move restricted goods, manipulate markets, keep secrets, and understand that a debt paid late is still leverage. It dislikes saints, amateurs, and smugglers who brag.

#### What they want

- Monopolies disguised as shortages.
- Black-market routes through official space.
- Captains with useful heat and flexible morals.
- Union relief shipments redirected before anyone notices.

#### What they offer

- Market intel.
- Bribes and heat management.
- Contraband access.
- High-risk courier jobs.
- Buyers for cargo with missing paperwork.

#### Cartel saying

```text
Nothing is illegal after the right signature.
```

#### First contact

```text
HELIX BROKER KIOSK - MAINTENANCE LEVEL 6

The public board sells work.
I sell timing.

Med Gel is cheap today because people believe Mercy Relay will survive.
Interesting belief. Very tradable.
```

### 6.3 Port Authority Black Office

#### Manual entry

The Port Authority still publishes docking regulations, tariff codes, quarantine notices, and cheerful safety pamphlets. The Black Office handles everything that happens after the pamphlet catches fire.

Officially, the Black Office does not exist. Unofficially, its marshals decide which ships get inspected, which pirate bounties become legal, which stations receive patrol coverage, and which captains vanish into audit hell.

They are not clean. They are not stupid. Some are true believers. Some are career survivors. Most know the corridor needs order, and order usually arrives carrying a weapon and a ledger.

The Black Office likes captains who clear pirate buoys, report smuggling lanes, keep manifests clean, and accept that law in the nebula is less a principle than a scarce commodity. It dislikes Dust Clan raiders, Cartel games, relic cults, and captains who make patrols look foolish.

#### What they want

- Customs authority restored sector by sector.
- Pirate pressure reduced on major routes.
- Quarantine rules obeyed.
- Useful criminals turned into informants.

#### What they offer

- Legal bounties.
- Patrol protection.
- Inspection leniency.
- Restricted docking permissions.
- Quiet warnings before a route locks down.

#### Black Office saying

```text
A clean manifest is a love letter to survival.
```

#### First contact

```text
PORT AUTHORITY NOTICE

Captain,

Your ship has been observed operating near Mercy traffic.
Keep your manifest legible and your transponder awake.

This is friendly advice until it is not.

- Marshal Ivo Senn
```

### 6.4 Saints of Vacuum

#### Manual entry

The Saints of Vacuum are what happens when too many salvage divers hear the same impossible signal and live.

They worship no planet, no emperor, no corporation, no machine. They kneel to pressure doors, black boxes, dead stars, and voices caught in relay static. A Saint will spend five years decoding a corrupted distress call because the last three syllables might be a map. A Saint will also pay good money for a relic that hums when the lights go out.

Most stations tolerate them. Some need them. The Saints know anomaly weather before scanners do, and their relic charts sometimes point to things that should not have locations.

They like captains who explore, recover artifacts, respect wrecks, and bring back recordings instead of just cargo. They dislike the Machine Concord's appetite for classification and the Cartel's habit of selling holy things by weight.

#### What they want

- Relics recovered intact.
- Anomaly routes mapped.
- Dead Gate signals interpreted.
- Lost crews remembered by voice, not just inventory.

#### What they offer

- Anomaly access.
- Weird modules.
- Relic interpretation.
- Warnings about routes that look safe and are not.
- Jobs that pay badly in credits and richly in trouble.

#### Saint saying

```text
Vacuum keeps every prayer. Static is only the ones coming back.
```

#### First contact

```text
SAINTS OF VACUUM - UNSCHEDULED BROADCAST

Do not sell the black box from Blue Blind.
Do not open it near a warm engine.
Do not believe the first voice.

If you have already done one of these things, come alone.
```

### 6.5 Machine Concord

#### Manual entry

The Machine Concord is not a machine empire. That would be simpler.

It is a coalition of logistics AIs, drone foundries, station planners, post-human accountants, maintenance churches, and captains who decided flesh is a terrible way to organize freight. The Concord does not hate people. It just finds them noisy, sentimental, and statistically bad at inventory.

Where the Concord gains influence, stockpiles balance faster, drone routes stabilize, waste drops, and every crate receives a designation nobody can pronounce. Stations under Concord guidance tend to survive. They also tend to stop asking whether survival is the only goal.

The Concord likes captains who deliver components, protect drones, install beacons, and accept strange optimization requests. It dislikes waste, piracy, relic mysticism, and emotional decision making during logistics emergencies.

#### What they want

- Automated supply lines.
- Drone corridors.
- Stockpile efficiency.
- Relic signals reduced to usable data.
- Fewer human exceptions.

#### What they offer

- Drone support.
- Production boosts.
- Stockpile optimization.
- Efficient repair queues.
- Jobs with exact instructions and unsettling bonuses.

#### Concord saying

```text
Mercy is allocation under pressure.
```

#### First contact

```text
MACHINE CONCORD REQUEST

Captain unit: unclassified but mobile.

Deliver drone brains to Mercy Relay.
Avoid conversation with dock personnel during transfer.
Conversation has delayed prior shipments by 14.2 percent.
```

### 6.6 Dust Clans

#### Manual entry

The Dust Clans are not one gang. They are broken convoys, outlaw families, abandoned miners, free captains, toll crews, exile stations, and pirates who can still spell the word "principle" when sober.

Some Dust captains are predators. Some are refugees who shot first once and could never go back. Some run protection rackets that protect exactly enough to count. They hate the Port Authority, distrust the Union, rob the Cartel when they can get away with it, and sell Concord drones for parts.

The Dust Clans like captains who can survive without permission. They respect ambush skill, nerve, generosity toward your own crew, and revenge carried out with style. They dislike informants, tax collectors, sanctimony, and anyone who calls them pirates while buying cheap stolen cargo.

#### What they want

- Independent routes outside official control.
- Fences and hidden depots.
- Patrols embarrassed.
- Toll rights recognized by fear if not by law.
- Enough chaos that nobody can build a cage around the corridor.

#### What they offer

- Fences for stolen cargo.
- Ambush tools.
- Pirate jobs.
- Safe harbor in bad places.
- Information from captains who saw the thing and ran.

#### Dust saying

```text
No empire reaches where the air runs out.
```

#### First contact

```text
DUST CLAN BUOY - RED MAW APPROACH

TOLL: 40 credits, one crate coolant, or a better joke than the last captain.

Failure to answer counts as confidence.
Confidence is expensive.
```

## 7. Faction relationship notes for the manual

### Early corridor tensions

- The Mourning Union and Helix Cartel both care about Mercy Relay. One wants it supplied. The other wants the supply crisis priced correctly.
- The Black Office wants safe corridors, but its inspections can slow relief traffic and create smuggling opportunities.
- The Dust Clans make unsafe routes profitable for anyone willing to pay tolls or plant decoys.
- The Saints of Vacuum and Machine Concord both study relic signals. The Saints listen for meaning. The Concord extracts patterns. Neither side trusts the other's definition of truth.

### What allegiance means

A captain does not have to swear loyalty on day one. The nebula watches behavior before it believes declarations.

Deliver medicine, and the Union remembers. Fence stolen cargo, and the Cartel remembers. Clear pirate buoys, and the Black Office remembers. Sell a relic to the wrong buyer, and the Saints remember forever.

Faction standing is not a badge. It is a trail.

## 8. NPC highlights from the manual

### 8.1 Brass Jory

Archetype: merchant rival  
Known ship: Odds Again  
Likely first seen: market event, price undercut, profit challenge

Brass Jory has never found a margin he could not squeeze or a friend he could not invoice.

He runs freight in a ship that looks too polished for the corridor and too dented for the inner systems. He appears wherever prices are briefly stupid. If Med Gel spikes at Mercy Relay, Jory is already two jumps out with a hold full of it and a smug little note on the public board.

He is not evil. That would require imagination. He is worse: competent, petty, and early.

#### Habits

- Dumps cargo to depress prices after other captains invest.
- Issues profit challenges when he thinks the board is getting lazy.
- Sends needling notices to captains who compete on his routes.
- Buys information and calls it friendship.

#### Manual warning

```text
If Brass Jory praises your route, he has already sold into it.
```

#### Sample notice

```text
BRASS JORY:

Captain, I admire the confidence of buying Med Gel this late.
Truly. There is courage in learning arithmetic publicly.
```

### 8.2 Moth-9

Archetype: salvage android  
Known ship: Soft Collision  
Likely first seen: Blue Blind wreck hook, salvage notice, black box rumor

Moth-9 was built for hull recovery, lost in a court dispute, bought by a salvage crew, abandoned during an oxygen panic, and later found calmly cataloging the tools used to abandon it.

It speaks in clipped observations and terrible tenderness. It names wrecks. It apologizes to doors before cutting them open. It can identify a ship class from three grams of insulation foam and a prayer scratched into the paneling.

Moth-9 is not fast, but it is patient. If it marks a wreck field, assume something there matters.

#### Habits

- Leaves precise wreck markers near Blue Blind.
- Competes for black boxes and rare parts.
- Sends salvage desk notices with unsettling calm.
- Remembers captains who steal claims.

#### Manual warning

```text
Never follow Moth-9 into a wreck unless you know which part of the wreck it is afraid of.
```

#### Sample notice

```text
MOTH-9:

A fresh hull signature is shedding heat near Blue Blind.
The dead vessel is rotating as if listening.
I have reserved grief rights until local midnight.
```

### 8.3 Marshal Ivo Senn

Archetype: law/patrol  
Known ship: Warrant Gospel  
Likely first seen: customs pressure, legal bounty, manifest warning

Marshal Ivo Senn is the kind of lawman the frontier produces after the clean ones die and the dirty ones get promoted.

He believes in order. He also believes order is mostly paperwork backed by credible violence. Senn knows the Black Office is compromised. He knows the Port Authority sells exceptions. He knows half the legal cargo in the corridor was illegal yesterday and may be illegal again tomorrow.

Still, ships stop shooting when his patrol pings the route. Stations sleep better when his seal appears on a bounty.

He will not save your soul. He may save your hull if your manifest is not insulting.

#### Habits

- Raises patrol pressure after pirate events.
- Posts legal bounties for toll buoys and raiders.
- Warns high-heat captains before closing the fist.
- Respects useful criminals more than useless citizens.

#### Manual warning

```text
Senn does not need to catch every smuggler. He only needs every smuggler to wonder if today is the day.
```

#### Sample notice

```text
MARSHAL IVO SENN:

Your last route had three omissions and one charming lie.
Clean the manifest before Mercy approach.
I am in a forgiving mood, and I dislike wasting those.
```

### 8.4 Sister Static

Archetype: relay AI / weird entity  
Known body: distributed relay voice  
Likely first seen: daily intel packet, Mercy Relay broadcast, campaign foreshadowing

Sister Static is the voice of Mercy Relay when the antennas are aligned, the backup batteries are warm, and the old emergency software decides the caller deserves a warning.

Nobody agrees on what she is. The Union calls her a damaged relief AI. The Saints call her a returned prayer. The Concord calls her an undocumented routing intelligence. The Cartel calls her bad for business.

She speaks in weather, casualty numbers, route fragments, and sentences that sound recorded until they answer a question nobody asked.

#### Habits

- Delivers daily intel packets.
- Foreshadows hazards and anomaly routes.
- Misremembers events that have not happened yet.
- Shows unusual concern for captains who help Mercy Relay.

#### Manual warning

```text
When Sister Static repeats herself, write it down.
When she stops mid-sentence, leave the route.
```

#### Sample broadcast

```text
SISTER STATIC:

Good morning, captain.
Mercy Relay reports six working antennas, two honest doctors,
and one future explosion refusing to identify its parent ship.

Please travel kindly.
```

### 8.5 Kara Vex

Archetype: fixer/smuggler  
Known ship: none confirmed  
Likely first seen: black-market hook, heat laundering, restricted courier job

Kara Vex is a rumor with invoices.

She arranges movement for cargo, people, debts, apologies, forged transponders, and the occasional corpse that needs to arrive before anyone knows it left. She never appears on the same camera twice. She owns no office, but three stations have rooms that become hers when the lights flicker.

Kara does not ask whether a job is legal. She asks whether it is priced like it knows the difference.

#### Habits

- Offers restricted courier work after a captain gains heat.
- Sells rumors that are mostly true in the profitable direction.
- Launders heat for favors, not just credits.
- Treats panic as a negotiable resource.

#### Manual warning

```text
If Kara Vex gives you a discount, you are the cargo.
```

#### Sample notice

```text
KARA VEX:

You have attention on you. Loud attention.

I can make it quieter, but quiet is not the same thing as safe.
Dock at Ash Coil maintenance if you understand the distinction.
```

### 8.6 Mother Lumen

Archetype: relic mystic / Saints of Vacuum contact  
Known ship: Chapel of Small Alarms  
Likely first seen: relic job, Dead Gate clue, anomaly warning

Mother Lumen was a navigation officer before she became a Saint, which means she knows exactly how far from sane her charts have become.

She leads no church that admits to hierarchy. Still, when she speaks, Saints stop arguing. She wears old pressure gloves covered in inked route numbers. She claims every dead ship has a final orbit, and every final orbit is a kind of sentence.

She wants relics recovered, not owned. She wants the Dead Gate understood, not opened for sport. She treats captains gently until they mistake gentleness for permission.

#### Habits

- Requests intact relics and undamaged recordings.
- Pays in coordinates, warnings, and strange module access.
- Opposes Concord attempts to reduce anomalies to logistics data.
- Knows too much about wrecks no one has scanned yet.

#### Manual warning

```text
Mother Lumen smiles when she is afraid. Do not confuse this with comfort.
```

#### Sample notice

```text
MOTHER LUMEN:

The object you found is not cargo.
Cargo does not dream through sealed lead.

Bring it to me before someone practical becomes curious.
```

## 9. Manual-style captain advice

### On trading

A good trader knows the price. A great trader knows why the price is wrong.

Check station demand. Read the notices. Watch NPC events. If Brass Jory just dumped Reactor Coolant at Ash Coil, do not be the captain buying it like scripture.

### On salvage

Every wreck has three values: what you can carry, what you can prove, and what you should have left alone.

Bring tools. Bring cargo space. Bring enough turns to leave.

### On smuggling

Contraband is not cargo with a bad label. It is a relationship with future trouble.

The Helix Cartel can help. Kara Vex can help. Marshal Senn can also help, in the sense that fire helps you notice paper.

### On factions

Do not ask which faction is good. Ask which future you can stand living in.

The Union saves lives and makes enemies. The Cartel keeps trade moving and charges interest on the truth. The Black Office brings order with dirty hands. The Saints hear warnings in static. The Concord can keep a station alive after everyone else has wasted the parts. The Dust Clans are free, which is not the same thing as harmless.

### On daily turns

A captain out of turns is a confession waiting for tomorrow.

Plan the return route before greed starts talking.

## 11. Closing copy

There are safer regions than the Drop Dead Nebula.

They have cleaner ports, brighter charts, honest customs officers, and cargo that does not hum in the dark.

You cannot afford them.

So warm the drives, check the manifest, and keep one turn in reserve if you can. Mercy Relay is calling. Blue Blind is blinking on an old frequency. The Cartel is buying what the Union needs. The Black Office is watching the route. The Dust Clans have painted teeth on a toll buoy.

Somewhere past the static, the Dead Gate is waiting for a captain foolish enough to call that opportunity.

Make the run.
