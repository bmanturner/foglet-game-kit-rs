# Drop Dead Nebula — Progression, Balance, Pacing, and Low-Pop Scaling Deep Dive

Status: Draft v0.1  
Primary milestone relevance: informs all milestones, explicit balance pass M12  
Purpose: Define how turns, credits, ships, risk, rewards, seasons, and population scaling should feel over time.

---

## 1. Design Intent

Balance should support the north star:

> A solo player should want one more run. A populated BBS should feel like others changed the galaxy while you were away.

Balance should:

- preserve short-session fun;
- reward planning and information;
- support multiple careers;
- prevent infinite solved loops;
- keep failure recoverable;
- scale for low population;
- give long-term goals without endless grind.

## 3. Turn Pacing

Default target:

- 30 Daily Turns;
- 5–15 minute normal session;
- one missed day can carry limited reserve if enabled.

Turn spend should prioritize:

- travel;
- scan;
- salvage;
- combat/risk response;
- major faction/outpost actions.

Trading may remain zero-turn or low-turn to keep markets fluid.

Questions to tune:

- How many meaningful decisions per session?
- How quickly can a player recover from a mistake?
- Can casual players miss days without falling hopelessly behind?

---

## 4. Credit Curve

Early game:

- clear profit from First Mercy Run;
- first upgrade within a few sessions;
- mistakes sting but do not trap.

Mid game:

- ship modules compete with cargo investment;
- repairs and Heat create sinks;
- salvage offers spikes;
- faction work may pay indirectly.

Late game:

- outposts, rare modules, corp projects, and campaign goals consume large resources;
- wealth alone should not solve everything.

---

## 5. Reward Types

Use more than credits:

- credits;
- cargo;
- intel;
- faction standing;
- Heat reduction;
- module access;
- route unlock;
- outpost progress;
- leaderboard score;
- NPC relationship.

This supports non-trader careers.

---

## 6. Risk/Reward Tuning

High reward should require at least one:

- more turns;
- scarce cargo space;
- route danger;
- Heat;
- faction consequence;
- stale intel;
- damage risk;
- expiry pressure.

Failure should usually cost resources, not erase the character.

---

## 7. Career Balance

Careers to support:

- Trader;
- Smuggler;
- Salvager;
- Explorer;
- Privateer;
- Faction Agent;
- Colonist/Industrialist;
- Pirate.

Each career needs:

- early viable action;
- midgame progression;
- late-game reason to exist;
- at least one weakness.

Do not let Trader be the only reliable money path.

---

## 8. Low-Pop Scaling

If low activity:

- increase solo-completable jobs;
- keep Markets moving with NPC flow;
- scale shared goals down or add NPC contributions;
- reduce passive punishment;
- spawn salvage hooks;
- use NPC Challenges instead of player-only competition.

Avoid visible “easy mode” framing.

---

## 9. High-Pop Scaling

If high activity:

- increase job generation;
- prevent permanent stock starvation;
- add anti-monopoly pressure;
- make leaderboards seasonal;
- add more contested opportunities;
- rate-limit Notices/digests.

---

## 10. Season Pacing

Default season target:

- long enough for progression arcs;
- short enough for leaderboards/campaign to matter.

Candidate default:

- 4–8 weeks, adjustable by sysop.

Season phases should not advance solely by wall clock; they should consider world activity and Faction progress.

---

## 11. Balance Signals to Watch

- players run out of turns before making meaningful choices;
- players ignore jobs because trading pays too well;
- players ignore trading because jobs pay too well;
- salvage is too risky or too profitable;
- Heat either never matters or prevents play;
- Markets become dead/empty too often;
- low-pop shared goals never complete;
- NPCs feel like cheaters;
- players hoard instead of acting;
- ship upgrades feel mandatory rather than expressive.

---

## 12. Recommended Balance Process

At each milestone:

1. Preserve First Mercy Run regression loop.
2. Run a solo 7-day imagined/playtest arc.
3. Run a two-player async scenario.
4. Check whether old systems still matter.
5. Adjust rewards before adding new complexity.
6. Add UI explanation for any new penalty.
